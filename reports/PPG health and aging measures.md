# Beat timing delivers now; vascular age waits

The best-evidenced health measures need only beat timing or slow (below 1 Hz) signal content, and the no-SDK tier already supports them: the Watch6 Classic's continuous ~25 Hz `hr_raw` stream plus its 100 Hz accelerometer, recorded overnight, yields **nightly resting heart rate** (all-cause mortality RR 1.09 per 10 bpm across 46 cohorts), **HRV read against age and sex norms** (our GalaxyPPG benchmark measured resting RMSSD within a median ~3 ms of ECG), **overnight respiratory rate** (Samsung's own 25 Hz pipeline reaches 1.13 breaths/min RMSE against polysomnography), sleep staging, an **atrial fibrillation (AF) screen** (wrist models reach AUC 0.95–0.97 at 20–25 Hz) and **illness alerts** (overnight heart rate alone flagged 80% of presymptomatic COVID-19 infections). The SDK tier adds what only single-lead ECG, skin temperature and 100 Hz multi-wavelength blocks can give: ECG-confirmed AF, pulse arrival time, cycle and fever tracking, SpO2 spot checks, and the waveform fidelity that pulse-shape measures need. Biological and vascular aging is where hype and evidence diverge most: Apple's PpgAge reaches a 2.4-year mean absolute error and predicts cardiovascular disease (hazard ratio 1.46 per 6-year gap), but only with 213,593 people, 30-day averaging and closed weights, while the best open encoders reach 6–10-year error and the one open age model collapses outside UK Biobank, so any "PPG age" we compute is a within-person trend, not a number to show users. Cardiometabolic outputs follow the same pattern, with hypertension and diabetes risk flags available (Apple's hypertension notification catches 41% of cases; a smartphone diabetes model reaches AUC 0.77) while calibration-free blood pressure, glucose values and lipids from PPG fail every independent check. Phone cameras split cleanly: fingertip video supports heart rate, HRV (with sub-frame interpolation) and AF spot checks, and face video supports only pulse and breathing rate, the only face measures the FDA has cleared. Licences limit the toolset as much as accuracy does, because Pulse-PPG (MIT, pretrained on Wear OS watch PPG) is the one clean open encoder for our watch, PaPaGei's weights and AnyPPG carry no licence, pyPPG is "academic research only" GPL, rPPG-Toolbox forbids inferring health conditions, and FacePhys weights forbid server-side use. The build order follows directly: check `hr_raw` against the watch ECG and prove that all-night logging works, ship the nightly beat-timing pipeline, then add daily 30-second SDK spot checks (ECG plus 100 Hz PPG) as the route to vascular trends.

## Four signal regimes decide what each device tier can measure

The useful question is not which health measure we want but which part of the pulse signal carries it, because each part has its own sampling and continuity needs and our devices deliver them unevenly. **Beat timing** (heart rate, HRV, rhythm) depends on placing each beat to within a few milliseconds, which signal quality and sub-sample interpolation achieve more than raw sampling rate. On the public GalaxyPPG dataset our two best 25 Hz methods estimated 60-second RMSSD within a median 3.0–3.1 ms of chest-strap ECG on held-out participants, while naive band-pass-and-peak-picking overestimated it by about 180 ms ([our 25 Hz HRV benchmark](../docs/hrv-25hz-benchmark.md)); a separate wrist-PPG study found performance statistically unchanged down to 25 Hz and about 5% worse at 20 Hz ([arXiv 2508.13690](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13690v1)). **Slow modulations** — breathing at 0.1–0.5 Hz, nightly temperature, resting baselines — need continuity rather than rate, and Samsung's validated respiratory-rate algorithm runs on exactly our no-SDK inputs, 25 Hz green PPG plus a wrist accelerometer ([Jung et al., Sensors 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10536355/)).

**Waveform morphology** is where 25 Hz breaks down. Most pulse-wave information lies in the first ~10 harmonics, below about 10 Hz ([Charlton et al., VascAgeNet review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8917928/)); at 25 Hz the Nyquist limit of 12.5 Hz cuts the 10th harmonic once heart rate exceeds about 75 bpm, and interpolation restores beat timing but not lost harmonics, which are exactly what defines the small c, d and e waves of the second-derivative pulse. Avram's diabetes model, for comparison, used 100–120 Hz fingertip PPG low-passed at about 10 Hz ([Avram et al. full text](https://www.gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/cnn/2020-avram.pdf)). The open foundation encoders band-limit their input to 0.5–8 Hz ([AnyPPG, arXiv 2511.01747](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01747v3)), so they accept upsampled 25 Hz data, but the morphology-heavy targets — vascular age, arterial stiffness, blood pressure — belong on the SDK's 100 Hz blocks or the fingertip camera. Because beats are not phase-locked to the sample clock, averaging many aligned beats from the 25 Hz stream could in principle rebuild a usable average pulse; this is untested and depends on `hr_raw`'s unknown anti-aliasing filter. **Multi-wavelength and electrical signals** — red/IR light for SpO2 and haemoglobin, ECG for rhythm confirmation, pulse arrival time and ECG-based AI — exist only in the SDK's on-demand trackers, which run in the foreground only, for at most 30 s and one at a time ([our model-feasibility check](../docs/model-feasibility.md)). No red/IR or ECG recording therefore runs overnight under our control, and overnight oxygen data has to come from Samsung's own outputs.

Four device facts shape every plan below. `hr_raw` arrives as 16 integer channels at about 24.9 Hz, partly decoded: v0 is wrist status, v1 and v4 the raw light level, v5 the AC pulse channel, which the server already uses ([model-feasibility check](../docs/model-feasibility.md)); the benchmark validated Samsung's SDK green stream on a Watch5, and `hr_raw` carries the same pulse (r ≈ 0.95) but has never been checked against an ECG. The SDK stream on GalaxyPPG **silently drops one sample every 10–12 s** while its timestamps stay evenly spaced, so one interval comes out about 40 ms short and inflates RMSSD unless corrected. Samsung's beat-interval (IBI) list lags each beat by about 6–7 s (7.4 s on GalaxyPPG's Watch5, 5.2–7.0 s on our SM-R965F) and needs the same cleaning, improving from a 10 ms median RMSSD error raw to 4.4 ms cleaned ([benchmark](../docs/hrv-25hz-benchmark.md)). Samsung Health's "download personal data" export holds 1-minute HR bins, HRV bins, SpO2, skin temperature, stress and sleep stages, and probably overnight beat-to-beat intervals (`rri_info`) for about the last week, the last point inferred rather than confirmed ([model-feasibility check](../docs/model-feasibility.md)).

## Nightly beat timing carries the strongest outcome evidence

**Resting heart rate is the cheapest measure with the hardest outcomes.** In a meta-analysis of 46 prospective cohorts, all-cause mortality rose by **RR 1.09 (1.07–1.12) per 10 bpm**, resting HR above 80 bpm carried RR 1.45 against the lowest category, and the relation stayed roughly linear above 45 bpm and unchanged after adjusting for traditional risk factors ([Zhang et al., CMAJ 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26598376/)). In 502,534 UK Biobank participants the hazard ratio per 10 bpm was 1.22 in men and 1.19 in women, stronger at younger ages ([PLOS ONE 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7259773/)). HRV adds autonomic information: **low SDNN carried about 40% higher risk of a first cardiovascular event** in 21,988 people without known disease ([Hillebrand et al., Europace 2013](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/handle/1887/102345)), and in 8.2 million Fitbit users high-frequency HRV power fell 82% in men between ages 20 and 60 ([Natarajan et al., Lancet Digital Health 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(20)30246-6)), a paper whose age- and sex-specific RMSSD tables let a nightly RMSSD be expressed as an age percentile. Heart-rate recovery adds a third independent signal, with attenuated post-exercise recovery carrying **HR 1.68 (1.51–1.88) for all-cause death** across nine cohorts ([Qiu et al., JAHA 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5524096)).

All three need only beat times or 1 Hz heart rate, so every wrist tier supports them. The caveat is that the outcome evidence comes from clinical or ECG measurement; no cohort linking consumer-wearable resting HR, HRV or HR recovery to mortality turned up, and free-living recovery after unplanned exertion is unstandardised. Fixed definitions — the lowest 5-minute mean HR of the night, RMSSD over motion-gated sleep windows — cut day-to-day noise and make nights comparable. These markers measure risk, not age: Apple's HR/HRV-only age model reached a 6.1-year error against 2.4 years for its waveform model ([PpgAge, Nature Communications 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12537950/)), and on clinical finger PPG, HR/HRV features predicted age barely better than the population mean (MAE 11.49 vs 11.91 years) ([Brag, arXiv 2603.14030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14030)).

**Cardiorespiratory fitness is worth tracking even though wrist estimates are imprecise.** In 122,007 treadmill-tested patients, low against elite fitness carried a **mortality HR of 5.04** with no upper limit of benefit ([Mandsager et al., JAMA Netw Open 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6324439)), and each additional MET of fitness corresponds to about 13% lower all-cause mortality ([Kodama et al., JAMA 2009](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2009.681)). Wrist VO2max is good for groups, not individuals: in the INTERLIVE meta-analysis, exercise-based estimates had near-zero bias but limits of agreement of about ±10 mL/kg/min, and resting-based estimates were biased by +2.17 with limits of about −13 to +17 ([Molina-Garcia et al., Sports Med 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9213394)). Samsung's VO2max has only a company-reported 82% correlation behind it ([Samsung Newsroom](https://news.samsung.com/us/galaxy-watch-shows-strong-correlation-with-clinical-and-sports-science-equipment-in-university-of-michigan-studies)). The Watch6's own 1 Hz HR is reliable during exercise, running just 2.67 bpm below a chest strap on average during maximal treadmill tests ([JMIR Cardio 2026](https://cardio.jmir.org/2026/1/e81917)), whereas every non-naive method in our benchmark declined all walking and running windows on the 25 Hz raw stream ([benchmark](../docs/hrv-25hz-benchmark.md)). Exercise-phase fitness should therefore come from Samsung's HR stream, or from a free-living model of heart rate against activity like that of Spathis et al., which reached r = 0.82 against exercise tests and tracked change over seven years ([npj Digital Medicine 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9718831/)), reported as a trend with about ±10% uncertainty.

**AF screening is the most mature disease application, and it is a beat-timing problem.** Apple's cleared algorithm classifies inter-beat "tachograms" and notifies when 5 of 6 are irregular within 48 hours ([Apple clinician document](https://www.apple.com/uk/healthcare/docs/site/Apple_Watch_Arrhythmia_Detection.pdf)); Fitbit requires 11 of 11 consecutive irregular tachograms, which gave a **98.2% positive predictive value** against ECG patches in 455,699 people ([Lubitz et al., Circulation 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/9640290)). Samsung's own Irregular Heart Rhythm Notification runs at a lower-sensitivity operating point, **68.0% sensitivity and 98.8% specificity** ([FDA K230292](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K230292.pdf)), and its ECG app reached 96.0% AF sensitivity and 98.7% sinus-rhythm specificity in 1,013 subjects ([K240909](https://fda.innolitics.com/device/K240909)). The sampling-rate evidence is reassuring: a deep model on 20 Hz PPG from a Samsung wrist device reached AUC 0.95 ([Stanford ML Group](https://stanfordmlgroup.github.io/projects/ppg/)), and on free-living PPG resampled to 25 Hz a deep network reached AUROC 0.973 while analysing 95% of the data, with a heuristic RR-interval method matching it (0.972) but usable on only 62% after motion rejection ([JACC: Clinical EP 2024](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacep.2024.01.008)). The screen can therefore run continuously on `hr_raw`, with the SDK ECG supplying the confirmation guidelines demand, since a single-lead ECG of ≥30 s is diagnostic after physician review ([RCM review of ESC guidance](https://www.imrpress.com/journal/RCM/27/10/10.31083/RCM51122)) and the 2023 ACC/AHA guideline is reported to hold that PPG alone is not enough ([Health Technology Wales EAR067](https://healthtechnology.wales/wp-content/uploads/EAR067-PPAF-0.27-CB.pdf)). The engineering risks are motion artefacts, ectopic beats (the main source of false positives) and the absence of any open model trained on Samsung 25 Hz green PPG; persistence rules like Apple's and Fitbit's are the cheap defence.

**Personal-baseline illness alerts work, with many false alarms.** The NightSignal algorithm, using only overnight resting HR, alerted for **80% (67 of 84) of presymptomatic or asymptomatic COVID-19 infections**, but stress, alcohol and travel also triggered it: 1.15 alert-days per person outside infection against 3.42 in cases ([Alavi et al., Nature Medicine 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8799466/)). Adding continuous skin temperature raised the multimodal TemPredict model's AUC by 4.9% to 0.819 ([Sci Rep 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35236896/)), and nightly respiratory rate rose ≥3 breaths/min in 36.4% of symptomatic cases against 4.3% in a control window ([Natarajan et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8443549/)). Vendor "readiness" composites are no model to copy: across 14 such scores from 10 manufacturers, Samsung included, none disclosed its formula and few offered validation ([Doherty et al. 2025](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/teb-2025-0001/html)). Transparent sub-signals — deviation from personal baseline in RHR, RMSSD, respiratory rate and temperature — are both more honest and easier to debug.

**"Stress" from PPG is a lab result that does not survive free living.** In WESAD's leave-one-subject-out evaluation, wrist blood-volume pulse alone separated stress from non-stress with **84.2% accuracy** and all wrist physiology together with 88.3% ([Schmidt et al., ICMI 2018](https://www.eti.uni-siegen.de/ubicomp/papers/ubi_icmi2018.pdf)); later claims of 97–99% are inflated by class imbalance ([arXiv 2203.09669](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09669)). In 781 students followed for three months, the Garmin stress score and self-reported stress "did not overlap for most individuals" ([Siepe et al. 2025](https://www.uni-marburg.de/en/fb04/team-heck/news/news/new_publication_about_ema)). Skin conductance, the modality that best separates arousal from physical load, is Galaxy Watch8-only in Samsung's SDK ([Samsung data specifications](https://developer.samsung.com/health/sensor/guide/data-specifications.html)). The honest output is "autonomic arousal relative to baseline", reported at rest only; the timing itself holds up, since our benchmark covered 84% of cognitive-stress windows at 3.3 ms RMSSD error ([benchmark](../docs/hrv-25hz-benchmark.md)).

## Sleep makes the watch a respiratory monitor, while overnight oxygen stays Samsung's

**Overnight respiratory rate is close to solved for trend tracking.** Samsung's algorithm fuses three PPG respiratory modulations with the accelerometer in Lomb–Scargle spectra and, in 195 sleep-clinic patients, reached **RMSE 1.13 breaths/min for the overnight average** and 1.62 for one-minute values, degrading to 1.74 in severe apnea and improving to 0.46 overnight when AHI was below 30 ([Jung et al., Sensors 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10536355/)). Fitbit's method reads respiratory sinus arrhythmia from beat intervals in deep sleep, reaching MAE 0.46 breaths/min, and supplies a population norm of 15.4 breaths/min (SD 2.35) from 10,000 users ([Natarajan et al., npj Digit Med 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8443549/)). Awake spot checks are another matter: the best of 314 published algorithms, run on 500 Hz research signals from resting young adults, had 95% limits of −5.1 to +7.2 breaths/min and produced an estimate for only 54% of windows ([Charlton et al., Physiol Meas 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5390977/)). The no-SDK tier mirrors Samsung's validated inputs and the SDK's IBI list enables the Fitbit-style method, so the product is the nightly median rate; daytime respiratory rate is not worth promising.

**Sleep staging has a ceiling set by the commercial devices and a higher one set by open research models.** Against polysomnography the Galaxy Watch5 reached **four-stage κ 0.418** and 64.9% accuracy, level with Fitbit and Pixel ([Lee et al., JMIR mHealth 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10654909/)), and the Apple Watch Series 8 reached κ 0.53 in a separate study ([Schyvens et al., SLEEP Advances 2025](https://academic.oup.com/sleepadvances/article/6/2/zpaf021/8090472)). Open raw-waveform models do better on finger PPG: SleepPPG-Net reached κ 0.75 on MESA ([arXiv 2202.05735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.05735)), and wav2sleep, which publishes code and weights, reached **PPG-only κ 0.742–0.832** across NSRR cohorts against 0.66 for a beat-interval-only baseline ([Carter & Tarassenko, ML4H 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04644v1)). The one external wrist test, on a wrist-worn oximeter, gave κ 0.66–0.67 ([SleepPPG-Net2, arXiv 2404.06869](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06869v1)). Both open models resample PPG to about 34 Hz, so upsampled 25 Hz input is plausible; the real risk is domain shift from finger infrared to wrist green PPG, which fine-tuning on DREAMT — 100 sleep-clinic patients wearing an Empatica E4 with simultaneous polysomnography, under a restricted data-use agreement — can address ([PhysioNet DREAMT](https://physionet.org/content/dreamt/)). wav2sleep's licence is unstated and must be checked before deployment, and Samsung's own stages in the export are the baseline any of our models must beat.

**Sleep apnea screening depends on which signals are available.** Samsung's cleared feature, available on Galaxy Watch4 and later running Wear OS 5, counts SpO2 desaturations with actigraphy over 2 nights within 10 days and reached **82.7% sensitivity and 87.7% specificity** for moderate-to-severe obstructive apnea in 470 subjects; it is a one-off screen, not a monitor ([Samsung IFU](https://image-us.samsung.com/SamsungUS/home/apps/samsung-health-monitor/IFU_SLEEP-APNEA_US_v1.0_2024-12_v1.2.pdf)). Apple's cleared feature uses the accelerometer alone over 30 days and reached 66.3% sensitivity (43% for moderate, 89% for severe) at 98.5% specificity ([FDA K240929](https://fda.innolitics.com/device/K240929)). Green-only wrist PPG at 32 Hz plus accelerometer, with no SpO2, estimated AHI with Spearman ρ 0.67 and, at AHI ≥15, reached 62% sensitivity and 91% specificity ([Papini et al., Sci Rep 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7421543/)). Because the SDK confines red/IR to 30-second foreground blocks, we cannot count overnight desaturations ourselves; real screening should use Samsung's feature (its regional availability is unverified), while a green-plus-accelerometer AHI estimate is a research track that reuses our respiratory-rate and IBI pipelines. Wrist SpO2 itself is a trend, not a measurement: the Galaxy Watch4 sampled red and IR at 25 Hz per wavelength and reached **1.8% RMSD** against arterial co-oximetry in a 12-person hypoxia lab study after rejecting 10.2% of readings ([Browne et al., Sensors 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11902706/)), and in hospital patients other watches lost 28–31% of readings ([Jiang et al., PLOS Digit Health 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10337940/)).

**Skin temperature is the SDK's quiet contribution.** Apple estimated ovulation retrospectively from wrist temperature with an MAE of about 1.2 days, **89% within ±2 days** ([Apple support](https://support.apple.com/120357)); the Galaxy Watch5 placed progesterone-confirmed ovulation within 2 days in 64% of cycles, a figure available only from a search snippet ([SKKU record](https://scholarx.skku.edu/item/c076f260-522c-43cb-a0fd-3d330decb736)); and Samsung's Cycle Tracking uses Natural Cycles' temperature algorithm ([GSMArena](https://m.gsmarena.com/samsung_galaxy_watch5_watch5_pro_temperature_based_cycle_tracking-news-57538.php)). The luteal phase raises both resting HR and temperature, so cycle-phase modelling must run before any illness alert in menstruating users, or the alert will fire monthly.

## Aging clocks need waveform shape, scale and averaging we lack

**The best PPG aging clock is real, closed, and built on scale.** Apple's PpgAge took 60-second, four-channel green wrist segments sampled at 64 or 256 Hz during low-motion periods, embedded them with a self-supervised encoder pretrained on about 20 million segments, and fitted a *linear* ridge model to the mean embedding over a 30-day window of at least 30 segments. It reached **MAE 2.42–2.45 years** in healthy participants and 3.13–3.26 years in the general held-out cohort of 120,235, and each 6-year gap carried **HR 1.464 for incident atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease** and 1.620 for hypertension after adjusting for age, sex, BMI, VO2max, smoking, blood pressure, cholesterol and diabetes. The gap moved with smoking, exercise and deep sleep (−0.99 years per extra hour of deep sleep), but the study is observational with self-reported diagnoses, at most about 4 years of follow-up, no mortality data, no external validation and no released weights ([Miller et al., Nature Communications 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12537950/)). The same group's earlier foundation model read age with MAE 3.19 years from wrist PPG against 6.33 years from ECG, with participant-level AUCs of 0.866 for diabetes, 0.819 for hypertension and 0.818 for AF ([Abbaspourazad et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05409v2)), which proves that wrist green PPG at 64 Hz encodes age and cardiometabolic status even though nobody outside Apple can use that encoder.

**OPPO's "heart age" is the closest analogue to our SDK spot-check mode.** From 7.45 million user-initiated 30-second recordings of 250 Hz, four-channel watch PPG in 581,804 people, a PPG-only model reached **4.95-year MAE**; error fell from 5.40 to 4.95 years as 1 to 15 recordings within 3 months were averaged and plateaued after about 10. In an external cohort of 560 people, the age-adjusted gap correlated with carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity (cfPWV, the reference measure of arterial stiffness) at partial r = 0.26, and each SD of gap carried an odds ratio of 1.72 for prevalent hypertension. It is a cross-sectional preprint with proprietary data and code ([arXiv 2608.01620](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01620v1)), but its protocol lesson transfers directly: about ten repeated 30-second spot checks per person per quarter reach a stable regime.

**Open models fall far short.** AI-PPG Age, trained on 212,231 UK Biobank participants' averaged 100-sample finger pulse, reached only r = 0.49 and **7.57-year MAE** on its own hold-out set ([arXiv 2502.12990v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12990v4)), yet its gap above 9 years still predicted major cardiovascular events with HR 2.37 ([Communications Medicine 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43856-025-01188-9)). An independent benchmark on 906 VitalDB surgical patients found AI-PPG Age's zero-shot error to be **11.5 years**, with "predictions collapse to a narrow 38–67 year range regardless of true age", while frozen Pulse-PPG embeddings with a ridge head reached 9.28 years, PaPaGei-S 9.80 years, and Pulse-PPG plus demographics 8.22 years, with error still falling as training data grew ([Brag, arXiv 2603.14030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14030)). The authors' own evaluations report age MAE of 7.78 years for PaPaGei ([arXiv 2410.20542](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20542)) and 6.08 years for AnyPPG ([arXiv 2511.01747](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01747v3)), and the UK Biobank ArtAgeGap model found hazard ratios of only 1.01–1.06 per year of gap over 13 years of follow-up in 96,615 people ([medRxiv 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.15.25340310)). A realistic open baseline is therefore 6–10 years MAE, which exceeds the 6–9-year gap thresholds the outcome studies use to call someone "older": open-model noise is larger than the effect. The defensible output is change within one person over long windows — PpgAge used 30 days, OPPO about 10 recordings — never an absolute age gap shown to a user.

**Classical pulse-wave indices have older, finger-based evidence.** The stiffness index (SI) correlates with cfPWV at **r = 0.65** with a 9.6% within-subject CV over a week, and Takazawa's aging index correlates with age at r = 0.80 in one cohort and 0.42 in another, although the a–c and a–d intervals it depends on repeat poorly (ICC < 0.5) ([Charlton et al. review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8917928/)). The second-derivative d/a ratio independently predicted cardiovascular death in 4,373 Japanese women, with **HR 2.30 and 2.60** for the upper quartiles ([Inoue et al., Hypertens Res 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/hr.2016.123)). UK Biobank's finger arterial stiffness index predicted CVD in 169,613 people (unadjusted HR 1.27) but improved risk reclassification by only 2.3%, against 5.4% for plain pulse pressure ([Said et al., JAHA 2018](https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.117.007621)). Nearly all of this evidence comes from finger PPG, mostly transmissive infrared, and the VascAgeNet review reports no wrist validation against cfPWV; one small conference study (n = 22, seen only as a search snippet) found the finger most accurate and, at the wrist, only green light behaving physiologically ([Sciforum 2026](https://sciforum.net/paper/26556)). For us the robust indices are the low-order features of an average beat — SI, crest time and the b/a ratio — while d/a, e/a and the aging index are fragile even clinically, and all of them shift with heart rate, blood pressure, skin temperature and contact pressure. The pressure-correcting model CP-PPG pushes every waveform toward a young-adult template and must never run before vascular features are computed ([model-feasibility check](../docs/model-feasibility.md)).

**Pulse arrival time is the SDK's distinctive stiffness signal.** Huawei's ECG+PPG watch, using a closed machine-learning model on 30-second measurements, agreed with reference cfPWV at **r = 0.918 and MAE 0.657 m/s** in 347 people, with errors widening above age 60 and at systolic BP ≥140 ([Sun et al., Front Cardiovasc Med 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9353553/)). Our watch can measure pulse arrival time from the ECG R-peak to the foot of the co-sampled green pulse, but that interval includes the pre-ejection period and travels a heart-to-wrist path that mostly bypasses the aorta. Under a phenylephrine challenge the pre-ejection period rose only 5.5 ms while transit times changed much more ([Finnegan et al., Sci Rep 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8611024/)), yet in ICU patients pulse arrival time correlated with systolic BP only at r ≈ −0.5 ([PMC6308183](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6308183/)). Pulse arrival time at a fixed posture is therefore a within-person trend, not a calibrated PWV, and whether the SDK's co-sampled PPG is precise enough for millisecond timing is unverified.

**Vendor features show the design pattern without the details.** Oura's Cardiovascular Age applies pulse-wave analysis to rest and sleep PPG over 14 of 30 days, and its estimated PWV correlated with cfPWV at **r = 0.83 in 99 people** in an unreviewed white paper ([Oura white paper](https://ouraring.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/FINAL_Oura_Cardiovascular_Age_White_Paper.pdf)). Samsung's Vascular Load analyses sleep-time pulse waves over at least 3 of 14 nights but runs only on the Watch8, Ultra and later, with no published validation ([Samsung UK support](https://www.samsung.com/uk/support/mobile-devices/health-features-on-the-galaxy-watch8-and-watch8-classic/)). Samsung's Antioxidant Index is fingertip carotenoid spectroscopy on Watch8 hardware ([Samsung Mobile Press](https://www.samsungmobilepress.com/feature-stories/galaxy-watch8-nutrition-tracking-sensor-antioxidant-index/)), and its AGEs Index is an unvalidated Watch7-and-later feature on which reviewers saw near-identical values across users ([Wareable](https://www.wareable.com/features/samsung-galaxy-watch-ages-index-explained)). Withings' PWV comes from a scale using ballistocardiography and foot impedance, not PPG ([Campo et al., Am J Hypertens 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajh/hpx059)), and WHOOP Age is an unvalidated composite of sleep, activity and fitness ([WHOOP](https://www.whoop.com/ca/en/thelocker/Healthspan-Data-Meets-Longevity/)). The pattern Oura and Samsung share — motion-gated sleep PPG aggregated over many nights and reported as a category relative to the person's own history — is worth copying on the no-SDK tier.

## Cardiometabolic outputs are screens and trends, never numbers

**Blood pressure is where expert bodies are most explicit.** The American Heart Association's December 2025 statement says cuffless devices are not accurate enough to diagnose hypertension or guide treatment ([AHA](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/cuffless-devices-for-the-measurement-of-blood-pressure)), and the European Society of Hypertension requires six separate validation tests for intermittent cuffless devices ([ESH 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37303198/)). Apple's cleared answer is a screen, not a number: a self-supervised encoder trained on more than 86,000 people feeds a linear head trained on more than 9,800 people with home-cuff readings, scores aggregate over 30 days, and the result had **41.2% sensitivity and 92.3% specificity** ([FDA K250507](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K250507.pdf)), meaning 59% of undiagnosed hypertensives get no alert ([Cohen et al., Hypertension 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12826266/)). Samsung's calibrated BP feature needs three cuff readings every 28 days ([Samsung support](https://www.samsung.com/au/support/mobile-devices/measure-bp-on-samsung-watch)), and independent tests found its accuracy falls as BP moves away from the calibration point ([Walzel et al., Digital Health 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/20552076261415923)). Calibration-free estimates fail outright: in a 2026 benchmark of 1,103 adults, **no state-of-the-art PPG-to-BP model met the AAMI/ISO standard** ([arXiv 2602.04725](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04725)), and Samsung's own HiMAE paper found BP regression poor for every model tested, its own included ([arXiv 2510.25785](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25785)). What we can honestly build is a within-person BP trend calibrated with a home cuff, driven by 100 Hz morphology and pulse arrival time from SDK blocks and reported as change from baseline; an Apple-style hypertension flag needs thousands of labelled people that a small cohort cannot supply.

**Diabetes risk rests on one large study, and its strongest clue is autonomic.** Avram et al. used 2.59 million smartphone fingertip recordings from 53,870 people, at 100–120 Hz and about 21 s each, and reached **user-level AUC 0.766** (0.740 in a later cohort, 0.830 with age, sex, race and BMI added), with 13% PPV and 97% NPV at 6.6% prevalence; a network fed **only beat intervals still reached AUC 0.721** ([Avram et al. full text](https://www.gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/cnn/2020-avram.pdf); [Nature Medicine 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-020-1010-5)). No independent replication or public weights turned up. The interval-only result means much of the signal is autonomic and within reach of our 25 Hz stream, but a risk score can at best nudge someone to get an HbA1c test. Glucose values are a dead end: the FDA warns that no smartwatch or ring is authorised to estimate glucose on its own ([FDA safety communication, Feb 2024](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safety-communications/do-not-use-smartwatches-or-smart-rings-measure-blood-glucose-levels-fda-safety-communication)), and when a wristband was paired with a Dexcom CGM the top predictors were clock time and the previous day's sugar intake, not physiology ([Bent et al., npj Digit Med 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8172541/)). Smartwatch data did detect hypoglycaemia with AUROC 0.76 in 22 insulin-treated adults ([Lehmann et al., Diabetes Care 2023](https://boris.unibe.ch/179063/)), so a CGM-paired N-of-1 study is a legitimate research track if a Dexcom is available, targeting postprandial excursions and nocturnal autonomic signatures and judged against time-of-day and meal baselines, with pretraining data in [BIG IDEAs](https://physionet.org/content/big-ideas-glycemic-wearable/1.1.2/) and [CGMacros](https://physionet.org/content/cgmacros/).

**ECG-based AI needs the SDK's ECG plus labels we do not have.** On Apple Watch lead-I ECGs, Mayo detected ejection fraction ≤40% with **AUC 0.88, but from only 16 positive cases**, using a model retrained on lead I with a 0.2–25 Hz band-pass and median beats ([Attia et al., Nature Medicine 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9805528/)); Samsung's Korean-approved equivalent runs only on the Watch8 and Ultra ([SamMobile](https://www.sammobile.com/news/samsung-bringing-potentially-life-saving-feature-galaxy-watches/)). A lead-I hyperkalaemia model reached AUROC 0.808 on external data ([Pocket-K, arXiv 2603.14177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14177)), and single-lead ECG flagged CKD with AUC 0.824 in people under 60 ([Holmstrom et al., Commun Med 2023](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s43856-023-00278-w)). Each is a model-training project on ECGFounder's MIT-licensed single-lead base that needs echo or lab labels to validate, so for us they are plumbing exercises. Two PPG results support logging embeddings to watch for drift rather than issuing diagnoses: Google's UK Biobank model, using fingertip PPG plus age, sex and smoking, matched an office-based cardiovascular risk score (C 71.1% vs 70.9%) ([Weng et al., PLOS Glob Public Health 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11149850/)), and AnyPPG embeddings reached AUC ≥0.70 for 307 of 1,468 phenotypes on held-out emergency-department data, including heart failure at 0.84, a single-cohort, non-causal result by the authors' own account ([arXiv 2511.01747](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01747v3)). No study supports lipids or kidney function from PPG.

## Phone cameras: the fingertip measures rhythm, the face measures rate

**Fingertip camera PPG is a capable spot-check instrument.** Google's study used a Pixel 3 at just 15 fps and reached **1.63% mean absolute percentage error** for heart rate, balanced across skin-tone groups, with 97.8% of readings within 5 bpm ([Bae et al., Commun Med 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9053269/)). HRV is where frame rate bites: a 33.3 ms frame interval quantises each beat time with an SD of about 9.6 ms (Δ/√12), giving about 13.6 ms of interval error and about 24 ms on successive differences, the same order as resting RMSSD. Sub-frame spline interpolation removes most of this, and the HRV4Training app, which upsamples camera PPG that way, matched multi-lead ECG rMSSD at **4.1% mean percentage error while another camera app was off by 112%** ([Frontiers in Sports and Active Living 2021](https://doaj.org/article/d59b87300e35405bba856079ad2a665b)); the algorithm, not the camera, dominates, and sensor frame timestamps must replace nominal frame indices. For AF, a meta-analysis pooled 94% sensitivity and 97% specificity across 6,891 participants, though no study met all quality criteria ([Gill et al., Heart 2022](https://heart.bmj.com/content/108/20/1600)), and FibriCheck held 96.3% sensitivity and 99.3% specificity across ten phone models ([PMC12635274](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12635274/)). The colour-ratio measures remain research: SpO2 from a desaturation study of six adults had 5% MAE, above the 4% accuracy bar for approved oximeters ([Hoffman et al., arXiv 2104.00038](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2104.00038)), haemoglobin reaches RMSE 0.81–1.39 g/dL in small studies ([BCU record](https://www.open-access.bcu.ac.uk/15484/)), and fingertip BP studies are small and developer-run, such as OptiBP with 50 participants ([PMC7576142](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7576142/)). Young and old could be separated at 30 fps with AUC 0.947, though covariates carried much of that ([Dall'Olio et al., Sci Rep 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7661535/)). Because the finger is the site of most classical morphology evidence and AnyPPG already reports results on 30 Hz smartphone PPG, the camera is our best morphology cross-check, provided colour-ratio work uses locked exposure, fixed white-balance gains, linear rather than tone-mapped output and per-phone calibration ([Hoffman et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2104.00038)). Third-party apps get about 30 fps on Samsung phones, and the 120/240 fps modes force auto-exposure ([model-feasibility check](../docs/model-feasibility.md)).

**Face video reliably gives pulse and breathing rate, and little else.** On easy benchmarks heart-rate error is below 1 bpm, but on MMPD — phone video from a Galaxy S22 Ultra across Fitzpatrick skin types 3–6, with motion and varied lighting — the best cross-dataset **HR MAE is 5.30 bpm** (FacePhys) and older models reach 9–13 bpm ([arXiv 2512.06275](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06275v1)). Google's passive front-camera study reached under 10% error against ECG across skin pigmentation ([arXiv 2503.03783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03783)), although errors have historically ballooned for Fitzpatrick type VI, 13.6 bpm against 3.4 bpm ([PhysFlow, arXiv 2407.21519](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21519v1)). Respiratory rate from torso motion in front-camera video reached 0.78 breaths/min MAE ([Bae et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9053269/)), but the best self-reported face RMSSD error, 16.45 ms, is about the size of many adults' RMSSD ([VitalLens 2.0, arXiv 2510.27028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27028)). Face BP fails the standard: in an independent-lab study under the ISO protocol, Lifelight's systolic error was **6.5 ± 12.9 mmHg**, well past ISO's 8 mmHg SD limit, and 40 participants were withdrawn because the app produced no reading ([Kapoor et al., JMIR Cardio 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11333865/)). The FDA's recent clearances cover only pulse and respiratory rate, for NuraLogix in June 2026 ([K253650](https://www.regdatalab.com/510k/K253650)) and Presage in July 2026 ([MedTech Spectrum](https://medtechspectrum.com/news/3/25750/presage-gains-fda-clearance-for-contactless-vital-signs-monitoring.html)); CE Class IIa marks for face BP exist (Shen.AI, Lifelight) but set a weaker evidential bar. "Facial age" comes from appearance, not pulse: FaceAge works from still photos ([Lancet Digital Health 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40345937/)), and the MCD-rPPG dataset's 3.91-year face-video age MAE most plausibly reflects appearance too ([arXiv 2508.17924](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17924v1)).

**Server-side processing changes what to upload.** Deep face-video models trained on lossless data break down under H.264/H.265 compression at **CRF ≥22** ([Sporrer et al. 2024](https://library.imaging.org/ei/articles/36/10/IPAS-248)), so the app should upload stabilised, losslessly encoded face crops with frame timestamps; ME-rPPG runs on 36×36 crops and the VitalLens API takes 40×40 ([ME-rPPG](https://github.com/KegangWangCCNU/ME-rPPG); [VitalLens API](https://www.rouast.com/api/)). For the fingertip, per-frame RGB channel means plus timestamps, exposure, ISO and torch state are enough, and the video itself is unnecessary. The server then adds what a phone cannot: an ensemble of classical and deep models gated by a signal-quality index, re-processing of archived traces as better models appear, cross-checks against simultaneous watch HR or ECG, and long personal baselines.

## Build the nightly timing pipeline first, then 30-second spot checks

The order weighs evidence strength against tier and against the engineering unknowns that could invalidate later work. Phase 0 settles four questions every later phase depends on — whether `hr_raw` times beats like the validated SDK stream, whether it drops samples, whether it can log a full night on battery under Wear OS background limits, and how much morphology survives at 25 Hz — and each answer needs one recording, not a study. The Samsung Health export should also be downloaded now, because its overnight beat-interval history is probably kept for only about a week.

| Phase | Build | Tier | Gate before moving on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Finish decoding the `hr_raw` channels (which ones are green, how heavily filtered); record 30 s of SDK ECG simultaneously with `hr_raw`; log a full night; record 25 Hz and 100 Hz side by side on the same wrist; take a Samsung Health export | Both watch tiers | `hr_raw` beat timing within benchmark limits; sample-drop behaviour known; a full night logged |
| 1 | Nightly pipeline: motion-gated sleep windows giving lowest-5-minute RHR, RMSSD/SDNN as an age/sex percentile, nightly median respiratory rate and sleep/wake; personal baselines with NightSignal-style deviation flags | No-SDK | Agreement with the export's HR, HRV and sleep values |
| 2 | Rhythm: tachogram AF screen with a persistence rule on `hr_raw`; on-demand SDK ECG confirmation via NeuroKit2 and ECGFounder | No-SDK, then SDK | False-alert rate on our own nights |
| 3 | Daily 30-second spot checks at a fixed posture: ECG plus 100 Hz green/IR/red for pulse arrival time, average-beat SI, crest time and b/a, and SpO2; nightly skin temperature for cycle phase and fever | SDK | At least 10 checks per quarter (OPPO's plateau); test-retest CV per index |
| 4 | Embedding trends: Pulse-PPG on the nightly 25 Hz stream (30-day means, PpgAge-style); AnyPPG/PaPaGei on 100 Hz blocks and fingertip traces; optional linear age probe trained on VitalDB/PulseDB and shown with its 8–10-year error | Server | Labelled exploratory; within-person change only |
| 5 | Phone: fingertip RGB-trace capture for HR, HRV and AF spot checks and finger-site morphology; face crops for HR and respiratory rate, with VitalLens as an outside baseline | Phone | Agreement with simultaneous watch ECG |
| 6 | Labelled research tracks: home-cuff BP trend; Dexcom-paired glucose excursions; DREAMT-tuned staging; green-plus-accelerometer apnea index | Mixed | Must beat naive baselines (time of day, persistence, calibration point) |

Phases 1 and 2 deliver the grade-A measures on the cheapest tier with permissively licensed tools throughout. Phase 3 is where the SDK earns its setup cost, because its ECG becomes a periodic reference that also calibrates PPG beat timing and its 100 Hz blocks become a repeated-measures morphology instrument run on the OPPO protocol. Phase 4 deliberately follows Phase 3, since embedding-based aging only means something once a long, quality-gated personal history exists to compare against. Six things should not be built at all: calibration-free blood pressure in mmHg, glucose values, lipids, face-video BP, SpO2 or age, carotenoid or AGE-type indices, and any absolute "vascular age" shown to a user.

## Conclusion

The gap between us and Apple is population scale, not sampling rate, which our 25 Hz stream largely survives for timing-based measures. PpgAge's accuracy came from 213,593 labelled people averaged over 30-day windows, and no public wrist dataset approaches that. Our counterweight is depth: continuous overnight data on the same person, with a periodic ECG reference, suits exactly the personal-baseline measures where the evidence is strongest — resting HR, HRV percentile, respiratory rate, rhythm and illness deviations. The product that follows is a personal-baseline engine rather than a population classifier, and that is also the only honest framing for aging, because open-model age error (6–10 years) is larger than the gap thresholds (6–9 years) that carry the outcome risk.

The SDK matters less for new measures than as instrumentation. Its ECG turns PPG-derived beats from "probably right" into "checked", and its 30-second 100 Hz blocks, repeated about ten times a quarter at a fixed posture, are the closest thing we can run to the protocol behind the best Android-vendor vascular-age result. The single most decision-relevant unknown is cheap to resolve: one simultaneous ECG and `hr_raw` recording, plus one same-wrist 25-versus-100 Hz recording, decide whether the no-SDK tier can carry HRV, AF screening and any waveform morphology at all.

## Updates after the report (2026-10-24)

- **Pulse shape at 25 Hz is less limited than stated above.** Our watch's 100 Hz green PPG keeps about 0.03% of its pulse power above 12.5 Hz, and on GalaxyPPG's 64 Hz wrist PPG the average beat rebuilt from 25 Hz samples reproduced crest time within about 1 ms and the second-derivative b/a ratio within 0.003. Averaged beats from the no-SDK stream may therefore carry pulse-shape indices; `hr_raw`'s on-device filtering and its hidden lost samples still need the same-wrist check ([phase 0 findings](../docs/phase0-offline-findings.md)).
- **`hr_raw` channels are mostly decoded** and its timestamps hide about two lost samples per 30 s, while the SDK stream shows its losses as gaps ([phase 0 findings](../docs/phase0-offline-findings.md)).
- **AF screen built and checked for false alerts.** The HRV cleaning would hide AF, so the screen uses its own beat settings and a trimmed RMSSD; on GalaxyPPG it raised no false alerts and flagged all synthetic AF. Sensitivity on real AF is still open ([AF screen](../docs/af-screen.md)).
- **Samsung Health data is a cheap first source.** Health Connect gives Samsung's heart rate, SpO2, sleep stages, blood pressure, glucose and workout VO2max; Samsung's Health Data SDK adds skin temperature, irregular-rhythm notifications, sleep-apnea results and Energy Score; the manual export adds HRV and stress. None gives raw waveforms or daytime beat timing.
- **Face video, graded by research rather than clearance:** AF screening from face video has been replicated by at least three independent groups in small seated studies (90–98% sensitivity, 91–96% specificity), and HRV is usable as a trend from still, lossless video. Blood pressure and SpO2 from the face remain unreplicated.
- **PPG age may be shown** with its error band and a warning, per the project's decision; the within-person trend stays the more reliable output.
- **CGM track:** a plan for an N-of-1 study with a CGM wearer is in [the CGM track plan](../docs/cgm-track-plan.md).
