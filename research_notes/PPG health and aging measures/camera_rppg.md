# Smartphone camera PPG: fingertip contact PPG and face-video rPPG — measures, accuracy, capture, open models, commercial SDKs

Scope note: state as of 2026-10-24. Extends `docs/model-feasibility.md` (ME-rPPG, FacePhys weight licence, AnyPPG, 30 fps on Samsung, 120/240 fps forcing auto-exposure), so those points are not repeated in depth. Disease detection (AF/diabetes/BP risk) is covered by another researcher; AF and BP appear here only as camera-measurement accuracy. "Older" marks results from before 2023 that have not been superseded by better evidence; "superseded" marks results that have been.

## 1. Fingertip camera PPG (torch, ~30 fps, sometimes 60 fps): what it measures, how accurately, and which capture settings matter

### Takeaway
Fingertip camera PPG reliably gives average HR (MAPE ~1.6% even at 15 fps in a 95-person prospective study) and, with sub-frame peak interpolation and a good app, resting rMSSD close to ECG. AF screening is accurate in pooled but low-quality studies. SpO2 (MAE 5% SpO2 in a 6-subject desaturation study), haemoglobin (RMSE ~0.8–1.7 g/dL in small studies) and BP (ISO-level agreement only in small, developer-run, often calibrated studies; ESH does not recommend cuffless devices) remain research-grade. Locked exposure, fixed white-balance gains, torch heat management and moderate contact pressure matter more than frame rate for HR, but frame rate (and timestamp accuracy) limits beat-to-beat HRV.

### Cited Findings

**Heart rate**
- Bae et al. 2022 (Google Health), *Communications Medicine* (DOI 10.1038/s43856-022-00102-x): index finger over the rear camera of a Pixel 3, **15 fps, 640×480, linear RGB**, 30 s recordings; ROI with highest SNR selected, channels weighted 0.67 R + 0.33 G + 0 B, band-pass 30–360 bpm, FFT peak; ROIs with SNR < 0 dB rejected. Reference Masimo MightySat. 95 participants (352 paired recordings), skin-tone subgroups balanced: **MAPE 1.63%** (subgroups 1.77/1.32/1.77%), 97.8% of readings within 5 bpm; first-try success 83.4%, 95.3% within three tries. Conditions included flash on, flash off in normal light, flash off in dim light, and post-exercise — [PMC9053269](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9053269/)
- A CinC 2026 study (Solinski et al., iPhone 13 mini, OpenPPG app with manual camera control) varied ISO, exposure, focus and white balance; lowest HR differences were at the longest exposure (E = 0.033 s; 2.64–2.80 bpm) and the highest ISO (400; 2.78–3.00 bpm) — [CinC 2026 abstract 512](https://cinc.org/2026/Program/accepted/512.html). (Only partial text retrieved; consistent with the 2–4.5 bpm contact-camera HR error from our prior check.)
- A 2014 IEEE conference study (Lamonaca et al.) concluded 30 fps is sufficient to extract the smartphone PPG signal for its features — [search summary of iris.unisannio.it record](https://iris.unisannio.it/handle/20.500.12070/13081) (older)
- A 2020 review: average HR and HRV from smartphone PPG are comparable to ECG, but most smartphone cameras sample at < 30 Hz, so fiducial-point detection can miss the true point — [PMC7181214](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7181214)

**HRV and the frame-rate problem**
- Choi et al. (cited in a PMC review): no significant HRV differences when PPG was sampled above 25 Hz (20-min resting recordings, subsampled) — but interpolation and protocol conditions were not considered; the same review reports parabolic and cubic-spline interpolation significantly improved IBI accuracy at 32, 16 and 8 Hz — [PMC8877143](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8877143)
- A numerical simulation of imaging PPG found only small errors beyond ~14 Hz when interpolation is used; tripling the rate to 50 Hz reduced error by only ~14% (SNR matters more than rate) — [Fleischhauer et al., via CatalyzeX](https://www.catalyzex.com/paper/signal-to-noise-ratio-is-more-important-than)
- A 2019 *Measurement Science Review* simulation concluded the minimum PPG sampling frequency for accurate HRV is higher than expected from signal bandwidth; RMSSD was the most demanding index, especially in low-variability series — [MSR 2019](https://www.measurement.sk/2019/msr-2019-0030.pdf)
- Low sampling produces jitter in pulse-to-pulse interval series that can considerably alter their frequency spectrum (LF/HF) — [Loughborough imaging-PPG PRV paper](https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/Noncontact_imaging_photoplethysmography_to_effectively_access_pulse_rate_variability/9572612/files/17206658.pdf)
- HRV4Training (Altini) resamples 30 Hz camera PPG to 180 Hz by cubic spline and validated rMSSD against Polar H7 across phones (vendor blog) — [HRV4Training Android blog](https://www.hrv4training.com/blog2/heart-rate-variability-using-the-phone-camera-android-edition); peer-reviewed validation: Plews et al. 2017, *IJSPP*, camera PPG vs Polar H7 vs ECG — [HRV4Training announcement](https://www.hrv4training.com/blog2/validation-paper-accepted-for-publication-in-the-international-journal-of-sports-physiology-and-performance-ppg-vs-polar-h7-vs-electrocardiogram) (older)
- Independent 2021 comparison (*Frontiers in Sports and Active Living*; 7 consumer devices vs multi-lead ECG, 148 trials, 5 young adults): HRV4Training camera rMSSD **MAPE 4.10%, CCC 0.98** (best of the devices), while the CameraHRV app had **MAPE 112.36%** — the algorithm, not the camera, dominates — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/d59b87300e35405bba856079ad2a665b)
- Vendor data, iPhone 16 Pro vs Polar H10, 8 min: rMSSD 75.1 ms (H10) vs 75 ms (camera) — [Altini Substack](https://marcoaltini.substack.com/p/extended-support-for-iphones-16-in) (single vendor comparison)

**Atrial fibrillation (measurement accuracy only)**
- Gill et al. 2022 meta-analysis (*Heart*): smartphone camera PPG vs ECG, 28 studies; pooled **sensitivity 94% (92–95), specificity 97% (96–98)** from 17 studies (6,891 participants, 2,299 with AF). No study met all QUADAS-2 criteria; selection and publication bias; authors called for independent, non-manufacturer-funded studies — [Heart 108(20):1600](https://heart.bmj.com/content/108/20/1600)

**SpO2**
- Hoffman et al. 2022, *npj Digital Medicine* 5:146 (DOI 10.1038/s41746-022-00665-y): varied-FiO2 induced-hypoxemia study covering SpO2 70–100% — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/2637ce72ffd3438193df03560ee8fbeb). Details from the arXiv version: **6 healthy adults** (12 hand recordings; 5 light skin, 1 dark), Google Nexus 6P, Camera2 app, **30 fps, 176×144, flash on, exposure locked at 1.2 ms, ISO 300, auto white balance off, fixed channel gains R 1×, G 3×, B 18×**, ice packs to prevent overheating; reference Masimo Radical-7. CNN on 3 s (90 frames) of mean RGB, leave-one-out. **MAE 5.00% SpO2** overall (best subject 3.14%, worst 8.56% — calluses); **SpO2 < 90%: sensitivity 81%, specificity 79%**; weaker below 85% SpO2; no motion filtering; ground truth from a transfer-standard oximeter, not arterial blood gas; several authors are patent inventors — [arXiv 2104.00038 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2104.00038). (Older; I found no larger follow-up desaturation study of fingertip camera SpO2.)
- Context: approved pulse oximeters must be within 4% (Arms) — [Cheng et al. 2024, Bioengineering](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10968547/); Hoffman's 5% MAE exceeds this.

**Haemoglobin**
- Public dataset of 1-min smartphone fingertip videos from 150 anaemic patients (Hb 4.3–12.4 g/dL): ResNet-18 **RMSE 0.81–1.39 g/dL** vs laboratory CBC; the earlier SVM-based HemaApp reached RMSE 1.7 on the same data — [BCU open-access record](https://www.open-access.bcu.ac.uk/15484/)
- 2018 AMIA study: 10-s fingertip videos from 75 adults, ANN on colour features, rank-order correlation 0.93 with lab Hb (range 7.6–13.5 g/dL) — [Hasan, AMIA paper](https://cs.mu.edu/~kamrul/amia_paper.html) (older)
- A 2025 medRxiv protocol (India) will test conjunctiva, tongue and nail-bed photos plus fingertip PPG against CBC across phones, noting skin-tone generalisability limits — [medRxiv 2025.04.10.25325591](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.10.25325591v1.full-text)

**Blood pressure (fingertip camera)**
- OptiBP (Schoettker et al. 2020, *Scientific Reports*): 50 participants vs auscultation (normo-, hyper- and hypotensive); mean difference ± SD **−0.7 ± 7.7 mmHg SBP, −0.4 ± 4.5 DBP, −0.6 ± 5.2 MAP**, within ISO 81060-2:2018 limits; a separate anaesthesia study of 121 patients retained only about half the recordings — [PMC7576142](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7576142/)
- CSEM oBPM (fingertip optical signal from smartphone camera), 85 hypertension-clinic patients, met ISO 81060-2 — [CSEM YODA record](https://yoda.csem.ch/items/ef59e226-0ecc-4427-9524-6ff968068d7d)
- AlwaysBP (2023): pulse transit time from camera + phone IMU, validated vs cuff in 87 participants — [PMC10503482](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10503482/)
- European Society of Hypertension (Stergiou et al. 2022, *J Hypertens*): ESH guidelines do **not** recommend cuffless devices for diagnosis/management of hypertension, and cuff-device validation protocols are inadequate for them — [Macquarie record](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/cuffless-blood-pressure-measuring-devices-review-and-statement-by/); 2023 ESH recommendations require six tests (static, device position, treatment, awake/asleep, exercise, recalibration) for intermittent cuffless devices — [Macquarie record](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/european-society-of-hypertension-recommendations-for-the-validati/)

**Waveform morphology / vascular age**
- Dall'Olio et al. 2020, *Scientific Reports* 10:19756 (not npj): smartphone PPG from the crowd-sourced Heart for Heart initiative; ridge regression on 38 features vs CNNs on whole signals; "healthy vascular ageing" classification **AUC 94.7%** with 2 features + 4 covariates, vs **95.3%** for a 12-layer ResNet; code released — [PMC7661535](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7661535/) (older)

**Capture settings**
- Hoffman: locked exposure (1.2 ms), fixed ISO, AWB off with fixed per-channel gains, 30 fps, torch on, ice packs against heating — [arXiv 2104.00038](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2104.00038)
- Bae: linear RGB at 15 fps, ROI search, SNR-gated rejection, 30 s; worked with flash on and off — [PMC9053269](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9053269/)
- Solinski (CinC 2026): longer exposure (1/30 s) and higher ISO gave lowest HR error — [CinC 2026](https://cinc.org/2026/Program/accepted/512.html)
- A TU Delft thesis reports that tuning camera settings plus finger detection gave PPG of 12.63% higher quality than a dedicated PPG sensor, noting the literature lacked a proper analysis of camera settings — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_0ee8f6f6-80ac-4f61-82a4-2c2412bff2a2)
- CamPressID (fingertip biometrics) notes prior work did not optimise camera parameters and finger pressure jointly, causing distorted/unstable PPG; it searches per-subject optimal pressure — [SMU record](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/sis_research/10380) (likely source; record not opened)
- Prior project check: third-party apps get ~30 fps on Samsung; 120/240 fps modes force auto-exposure; Chrome `getUserMedia` with torch and manual exposure is closer to validated setups — `docs/model-feasibility.md` (local)

### Inferences
- **Quantisation arithmetic at 30 fps** (my calculation, not a cited result): uniform timing quantisation of Δ = 33.3 ms has SD Δ/√12 ≈ 9.6 ms per beat, so IBI error SD ≈ 13.6 ms and successive-difference error ≈ 23.6 ms without interpolation. That is the same order as resting RMSSD (20–80 ms) and explains why naive 30 fps peak picking gives poor RMSSD while parabolic/spline sub-sample peak localisation (as HRV4Training does) gives near-ECG values. 60 fps halves this. Using sensor frame timestamps (not nominal frame index) is required because Android frame intervals jitter.
- HR is not frame-rate-limited (Bae: 1.6% MAPE at 15 fps); the 2–4.5 bpm errors in the literature are dominated by motion, pressure, ROI choice and exposure, not sampling rate.
- Our app's settings (torch, locked exposure and white balance, ~30 fps) match Hoffman's protocol; the missing piece is **fixed sensor gains / linear (non-tone-mapped) output** and heat control for longer recordings. Red/green/blue ratios matter for SpO2 and Hb, so any per-frame ISP tone mapping invalidates those estimates even when HR is fine.
- The fingertip-camera BP studies above (OptiBP, oBPM, AlwaysBP) are small and run by their developers; independent replication on current phones is missing.
- SpO2 and Hb from a fingertip camera need per-device calibration data (the torch spectrum and sensor response differ per phone model); a model trained on Nexus 6P data will not transfer to a Galaxy S Ultra or Honor without new desaturation/Hb-referenced data.
- Vascular-age AUCs that include covariates (likely age among them) overstate what the waveform alone adds; treat "vascular age" from a phone camera as an exploratory morphology feature tracked against a personal baseline, not an absolute biomarker.

### Gaps
- Whether Galaxy S Ultra / Honor expose 60 fps with manual exposure through Camera2 remained unverified (earlier notes say unconfirmed); no source found.
- Plews et al. 2017 rMSSD agreement figures were not retrieved.
- No large, independent fingertip-camera SpO2 desaturation study after Hoffman 2022 was found; no study on Samsung/Honor phones.
- No study found that jointly varies exposure/ISO and contact pressure and measures waveform morphology on a smartphone.
- OptiBP is believed to require a cuff calibration at initialisation; I did not confirm this from a primary source.
- Dicrotic-notch/second-derivative (APG) feature reliability at 30 fps camera sampling was not found in a direct study.

## 2. Face-video rPPG state of the art (2024–2026): HR on benchmarks and in the wild, robustness, HRV, respiration, SpO2, BP, facial age/risk scores

### Takeaway
UBFC-rPPG and PURE are saturated (HR MAE ≈ 0.2–0.5 bpm for the best 2025 models); MMPD (mobile phone, Fitzpatrick 3–6, motion, lighting) is the real benchmark, where the best published cross-dataset HR MAE is still ~5–8 bpm. In the wild, Google's large passive-smartphone study reached MAPE < 10% vs ECG across skin tones. Respiration from face/torso video is good (MAE ~0.8–1.1 breaths/min). HRV from face video is feasible only in still, well-lit, high-quality video (VitalLens 2.0: SDNN MAE 10 ms, RMSSD 16 ms). Face SpO2 and face BP are not credible for clinical use: SpO2 studies are tiny with little desaturation, and BP studies are vendor-funded with SDs (8–17 mmHg) above the ISO 81060-2 8 mmHg limit. Recent FDA clearances for face rPPG (FaceHeart, NuraLogix, Presage) cover only pulse rate and respiratory rate. "Facial age"/risk scores are vendor outputs without published validation, apart from photo-based FaceAge.

### Cited Findings

**Benchmarks (HR)**
- FacePhys (Wang et al., arXiv 2512.06275, Dec 2025; self-reported) — intra-dataset 5-fold HR MAE (bpm): **MMPD 5.58**, VitalVideo 0.64, PURE 0.26, UBFC 0.46; RhythmMamba 8.21 / 0.83 / 0.26 / 0.53; PhysNet 8.13 / 0.69 / 0.93 / 1.76; PhysFormer 9.47 / 0.66 / 2.66 / 5.42; TS-CAN 14.2 on MMPD; EfficientPhys 14.8 on MMPD — [arXiv 2512.06275v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06275v1)
- Same paper, **cross-dataset trained on RLAP**: MMPD MAE FacePhys **5.30** (R 0.76), RhythmMamba 9.62, EfficientPhys 9.18, TS-CAN 10.2, PhysNet 11.2, PhysFormer 12.8; VitalVideo FacePhys 0.77, PhysNet 0.89; UBFC all ≤ 0.9 — [arXiv 2512.06275v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06275v1)
- Same paper, **trained on PURE**: MMPD MAE FacePhys 8.45, RhythmMamba 10.9, PhysFormer 13.0, PhysNet 15.1, EfficientPhys 17.6, TS-CAN 18.1 — [arXiv 2512.06275v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06275v1)
- Efficiency (CPU): FacePhys 719 K params, 3.6 MB memory, 9.46 ms latency, 0.15 GFLOPs vs PhysFormer 7.4 M params, 2,330 ms; RhythmMamba 4.9 M params, 889 ms — [arXiv 2512.06275v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06275v1)
- Other 2025 papers report sub-1 bpm MAE on PURE/UBFC (e.g., 0.14 bpm PURE; 0.66 bpm UBFC unsupervised) and RhythmFormer MAE 3.07 on MMPD under an intra-dataset split; protocols differ, so cross-paper numbers are not comparable — [arXiv 2507.07795](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07795v1); [PHASE-Net arXiv 2509.24850](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.24850) (figures from search snippets; which number belongs to which paper was not verified)
- MMPD: 33 subjects, Fitzpatrick 3–6, mobile-phone video — [arXiv 2507.07795](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07795v1)
- VitalVideo: 893 subjects, 6 Fitzpatrick types, "largest real-world rPPG dataset" — [arXiv 2404.05003](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.05003)
- VitalLens 1.0 (Rouast): VV-Medium (289 participants) HR MAE 0.71 bpm, RR MAE 0.76 — [arXiv 2312.06892](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06892v2); **VitalLens 2.0** (Oct 2025), combined test set of 422 individuals from four public/private datasets: **HR MAE 1.57 bpm, RR 1.08 breaths/min, SDNN 10.18 ms, RMSSD 16.45 ms** (self-reported) — [arXiv 2510.27028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27028)
- A 2026 systematic review with deployment guidelines exists — [ScienceDirect S2590005626002924](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590005626002924) (not read in full)

**In the wild**
- Google PHRM (Liao, Poh, McDuff, Patel et al., arXiv 2503.03783, 2025): passive HR from the front camera during everyday phone use; development 225,773 videos / 495 participants; validation **185,970 videos / 205 participants** in lab and free-living; **HR MAPE < 10% vs ECG** across light/medium/dark pigmentation (non-inferior between groups); daily resting HR MAE < 5 bpm vs a wearable — [arXiv 2503.03783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03783). A search summary attributes Fitzpatrick-VI MAEs of 5.2 bpm (controlled) and 5.81 bpm (free-living) to this preprint — [arXiv 2503.03783 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.03783) (not verified in full text)
- Older commercial-tool validation (Noldus FaceReader rPPG, 2019): overestimated low HR and underestimated high HR vs ECG; judged unreliable for individual monitoring — [PMC6874325](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6874325/) (older/superseded)

**Skin tone**
- Nowara et al. 2020 meta-analysis (as cited by PhysFlow): MAE ~3.4 bpm for Fitzpatrick I–V vs **13.6 bpm for type VI**; PhysFlow (2024) generates darker-skin training videos and reduces MAE by 1–5 bpm on MMPD types V–VI — [PhysFlow arXiv 2407.21519](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21519v1)
- A 2023 benchmark of a commercial rPPG SDK (90 patients) reported ~3 bpm MAE across skin-tone groups — [Cureus 2023](https://assets.cureus.com/uploads/original_article/pdf/150278/20230912-4180-vpq056.pdf)

**Video compression (critical for server-side processing)**
- McDuff et al. 2017 (IEEE FG): x264/x265 compression degraded BVP SNR even at low CRF; roughly linear bitrate–SNR relation up to CRF 36; x265 better for motion — [Microsoft Research PDF](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2020/11/McDuff_2017_Impact2.pdf)
- Nowara, McDuff, Veeraraghavan 2020 (*Biomedical Optics Express*): pulse easy to recover from uncompressed video; SNR drops sharply as bitrate falls — [PMC7899506](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7899506)
- Sporrer et al. 2024 (Electronic Imaging): deep rPPG models trained on lossless data broke down on H.264/H.265 at **CRF ≥ 22** — [IS&T library](https://library.imaging.org/ei/articles/36/10/IPAS-248)
- ICIP 2026: H.266/VVC at 1080p performed best among codecs; spatial resolution had only a marginal effect — [visual-computing.com](https://visual-computing.com/publications/a-study-of-resolution-aware-video-compression-for-remote-photoplethysmography)

**HRV from face video**
- VitalLens 2.0: SDNN MAE 10.18 ms, RMSSD MAE 16.45 ms (422 people, self-reported) — [arXiv 2510.27028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27028)
- Shen.AI pilot (medRxiv 2025): 38 healthy volunteers, smartphone face video vs simultaneous ECG for SDNN and lnRMSSD; reported low errors and strong correlations, small seated healthy sample, larger studies recommended — [medRxiv 2025.02.13.25322028](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.13.25322028.full.pdf) (full text blocked; exact numbers not retrieved)

**Respiration**
- Bae et al. 2022: front camera (Pixel 4), face + upper torso, 15 fps, 30 s; optical-flow vertical motion of the torso, 6–60 breaths/min band; 50 participants (40 with asthma/COPD): **MAE 0.78 ± 0.61 breaths/min** (0.70 healthy, 0.80 chronic) vs manual counting — [PMC9053269](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9053269/)
- FaceHeart RR (FDA K243966): deviations within ±2 breaths/min across devices — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/asia/taiwanese-ai-company-obtains-510k-video-based-respiratory-rate-gauge); Presage: RR RMSE 1.75 breaths/min (company) — [MedTech Spectrum](https://medtechspectrum.com/news/3/25750/presage-gains-fda-clearance-for-contactless-vital-signs-monitoring.html)

**SpO2 from face**
- Cheng et al. 2024 (*Bioengineering*): deep model on public VIPL-HR, MAE 1.274%, RMSE 1.71% — [PMC10968547](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10968547/)
- CL-SPO2Net (2024): MAE 0.85% stable, 1.13% lighting fluctuation, 1.20% head rotation — [Bioengineering 2024 via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/1ada87bdcbb64740ba35f84111eb1ed6)
- DTU: smartphone front camera, Arms 1.34% over SpO2 92–99%, 10 healthy subjects, ideal lighting — [DTU Orbit](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/remote-estimation-of-peripheral-oxygen-saturation-and-pulse-rate-/)
- Samsung Research (EMBC 2023): consumer RGB camera, RMSE 1.93% under favourable lighting, desaturation to 81%, **2 subjects** — [Samsung Research](https://research.samsung.com/research-papers/Estimating-SpO2-with-Deep-Oxygen-Desaturations-from-Facial-Video-Under-Various-Lighting-Conditions-A-Feasibility-Study)
- Most face-SpO2 methods are evaluated on private datasets, preventing fair comparison — [PMC10968547](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10968547/)

**Blood pressure from face video**
- NuraLogix TOI (Luo et al. 2019, *Circ Cardiovasc Imaging* 12:e008857; co-founder Kang Lee is an author): 2-min iPhone videos, 1,328 **normotensive** adults (SBP 100–139), 70/15/15 split; bias ± SD **0.39 ± 7.30 mmHg SBP, −0.20 ± 6.00 DBP, 0.52 ± 6.42 pulse pressure**; authors stated future work must test the 5 ± 8 mmHg criterion over a full BP range — [x-mol abstract](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/5801687); [TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/smartphone-selfie-may-reveal-bp-levels-early-results-suggest). Press "95–96% accuracy" is not an agreement metric — [Engadget 2019](https://www.engadget.com/2019-08-07-blood-pressure-selfie-video-smartphone-research.html) (older)
- Medaval rates Anura 2 stars for BP, citing Yang et al. 2020 (*Blood Pressure Monitoring*, "preliminary assessment") under ANSI/AAMI/ISO 81060-2:2013 — [Medaval](https://medaval.ie/resources/EN/devices/NuraLogix-Anura.php); NCT04539860 (U of T + NuraLogix) last updated Sept 2020, no results posted — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04539860)
- **Lifelight (Xim Ltd, UK)**, JMIR Cardio 2024 (Kapoor et al.), run at an independent lab under ISO 81060-2:2018/AMD1:2020, 85 participants (BP analysis on 65 / 185 measurements): **SBP mean error 6.5 ± 12.9 mmHg (MAE 11.3); DBP 0.4 ± 10.6 (MAE 8.6)**; pulse rate bias 0.7 ± 1.1 bpm vs ECG; hypertension classification sensitivity 70.1%, specificity 71.7%; **44 participants withdrawn, 40 because the app could not produce a BP reading**; only 20% Fitzpatrick 5–6; controlled LED lighting; 30 fps 1080p; funded by Xim, which reviewed the manuscript — [PMC11333865](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11333865/)
- Lifelight VISION-D (larger development dataset): DBP −0.4 ± 8.5 mmHg, SBP 3.5 ± 16.8 — [Portsmouth research portal](https://researchportal.port.ac.uk/en/publications/measurement-of-vital-signs-using-lifelight-remote-photoplethysmog/); VISION-MD (NCT04763746) protocol, results not found — [JMIR Res Protoc 2023](https://www.researchprotocols.org/2023/1/e41533); NICE MIB213 (2020) relied on an unpublished, non-peer-reviewed study — [NICE MIB213](https://www.nice.org.uk/advice/mib213/chapter/Clinical-and-technical-evidence)
- Binah.ai company report (SDK 5.9.1, Mar 2025, India + Italy): SBP within 15 mmHg in 87.9% of cases; DBP within 10 mmHg in 82.9% — not peer-reviewed — [Binah report PDF](https://www.binah.ai/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/MED-000025-Blood-Pressure-Accuracy-Report-of-SDK-5.9.1_Rev2-Final.pdf)
- Vastmindz-affiliated Cureus 2022 study: ±10 mmHg SBP/DBP; framed as wellness-grade — [PMC9760129](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9760129/)
- Academic: rPPG BP model with SBP mean error 1.2, SD 15.9 mmHg — [Springer Discover Applied Sciences 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42452-024-05848-8); 2025 XGBoost face-rPPG BP claiming AAMI/BHS grade A (4.89 ± 6.62 SBP) — [PubMed 41114895](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41114895/) (single study, protocol not checked)
- ESH does not recommend cuffless devices for hypertension diagnosis/management — [Stergiou 2022](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/cuffless-blood-pressure-measuring-devices-review-and-statement-by/)

**Facial "age" and risk scores**
- FaceAge (Bontempi/Aerts et al., *Lancet Digital Health* 2025, 7(6):100870): **still-photo** deep model trained on 58,851 presumed-healthy people ≥ 60 (IMDb-Wiki, UTKFace); 6,196 cancer patients looked older than chronological age, and older FaceAge predicted worse survival; pretrained model distributed via NIH Biowulf — [PubMed 40345937](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40345937/); [NIH HPC](https://hpc.nih.gov/apps/FaceAge.html)
- NuraLogix Anura outputs include Vascular Capacity, Facial Skin Age, BMI, Body Shape Index, waist-to-height, 10-year CVD / heart-attack / stroke risk; some features labelled investigational-use-only in the US; no accuracy data published for these — [Anura Enterprise](https://www.nuralogix.ai/anura-enterprise); [MagicMirror release](https://nuralogix.ai/nuralogix-unveils-anura-magicmirror-creating-new-possibilities-for-health-measurement/)
- Binah SDK 5.9 added "Heart Age", High Fasting Glucose*, High Total Cholesterol*, Low Haemoglobin*, High HbA1c risk* (* = under research) — [Binah SDK page](https://binah.ai/sdk/); [Binah research](https://www.binah.ai/research/)
- Shen.AI exposes "age", cardiac workload, stress index, parasympathetic activity, BMI class, and computes CV risk from user-supplied risk factors — [Shen.AI developer docs](https://developer.shen.ai/video-measurement/results)

### Inferences
- For our use (still, seated, phone at arm's length), expect HR MAE ≈ 1–3 bpm with good light and a modern model, and 5–10 bpm on MMPD-like conditions or Fitzpatrick V–VI; this matches our prior 1.5–3 / 8–15+ bpm figures, with 2025 models narrowing the hard-case error to ~5 bpm.
- ISO 81060-2 criterion 1 requires mean ≤ 5 mmHg and SD ≤ 8 mmHg; Lifelight's published SBP SD of 12.9 and VISION-D's 16.8 fail the SD limit, Binah's "within 15 mmHg" metric is not the ISO metric, and NuraLogix's 7.30 SD came from a normotensive-only sample. Treat face-video BP as uncalibrated population-mean regression (it tends to predict near the mean BP for a person's age/BMI/sex), not a measurement.
- The pattern of FDA clearances (2023–2026: FaceHeart HR/RR, NuraLogix pulse/RR, Presage pulse/RR) shows that regulators accept face rPPG for rate-type measures only; CE Class IIa for face BP (Lifelight, Shen.AI) is a weaker evidential bar.
- HRV from face video needs uncompressed or near-lossless crops at ≥ 30 fps, still subjects and steady light; VitalLens' 16 ms RMSSD MAE is of the order of the RMSSD itself for many adults, so use rMSSD trends within a person, not absolute values.
- Pulse waveform morphology from face video is heavily filtered by models trained on HR losses; face-derived "vascular age" should be treated as unsupported.

### Gaps
- No rPPG-specific video foundation model with open weights was found (searches returned only contact-PPG foundation models: PaPaGei, Apple, Samsung MMR); masked-autoencoder pretraining exists (rPPG-MAE arXiv 2306.02301, Periodic-MAE arXiv 2506.21855, Semi-rPPG arXiv 2502.03855) but no large public pretrained checkpoint was confirmed.
- Shen.AI HRV pilot numbers (medRxiv 403) and the exact 2023 Dasari skin-tone figures (CHROM 5.2 → 14.1 bpm for darker skin, reported second-hand) were not verified.
- No independent (non-vendor) validation of NuraLogix BP, facial skin age or risk scores was found.
- No face-video SpO2 study with induced desaturation in > 10 subjects was found.
- MMPD/VitalVideo numbers for ME-rPPG under the same protocol as FacePhys were not in the FacePhys tables.

## 3. Open-source models and toolkits: weights, licences, and server-side usability

### Takeaway
For a FastAPI server on an RTX 4090, the cleanest options are: ME-rPPG (MIT repo, ONNX weights), RhythmFormer and PhysFormer (MIT code), pyVHR (GPL-3.0; fine for internal server use) and classical POS/CHROM/LGI/OMIT. rPPG-Toolbox carries a Responsible AI (RAIL) licence whose clauses forbid inferring "health and medical conditions" (surveillance clause) and diagnosing without human oversight, and its released weights fall under the same licence. open-rppg is MIT for code only; its weights, including the default FacePhys model, follow the original authors' terms (FacePhys forbids server-side inference). PhysMamba has no licence file. VitalLens' open client is MIT but the model is a paid cloud API.

### Cited Findings
- **rPPG-Toolbox** (ubicomplab; NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks, arXiv 2210.00716; ~1,235 stars; last push Sep 2025): licence is the **Responsible Artificial Intelligence Source Code License v1.1** (GitHub shows NOASSERTION). §3.2 restricts use to "Detect or infer … health and medical conditions" (surveillance clause; age and medical conditions allowed only for accessibility, and not to be stored without Licensor's permission), insurance uses, "Diagnose a medical condition without human oversight", and criminal prediction; restrictions must be passed on to downstream users — [LICENSE](https://github.com/ubicomplab/rPPG-Toolbox/blob/main/LICENSE); [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.00716)
- rPPG-Toolbox `final_model_release/` ships weights for DeepPhys, TS-CAN, EfficientPhys, PhysNet, PhysFormer, **PhysMamba, RhythmFormer, FactorizePhys (NeurIPS 2024), iBVPNet**, BigSmall, trained on PURE, UBFC-rPPG, SCAMPS (synthetic), iBVP, BP4D+, and motion-augmented UBFC; unsupervised ICA, POS, CHROM, GREEN, LGI, PBV, OMIT; produces Bland-Altman plots; supports MMPD subset selection by skin tone/motion/light — [repo README](https://github.com/ubicomplab/rPPG-Toolbox)
- **open-rppg** (KegangWangCCNU; `pip install open-rppg`; JAX, CUDA optional; last push Jan 2026): code MIT, but "pretrained models and model configurations … are licensed under the terms specified by the original papers"; default model `FacePhys.rlap`; model zoo ME-chunk, ME-flow, PhysMamba, RhythmMamba, PhysFormer, TSCAN, EfficientPhys, PhysNet, FacePhys (`.rlap`/`.pure` weights); `process_video` returns HR (FFT), **SQI 0–1**, and HRV (IBI, SDNN, RMSSD, pNN50, LF/HF, breathing rate); accepts uint8 tensors with explicit fps — [open-rppg README/LICENSE](https://github.com/KegangWangCCNU/open-rppg)
- **ME-rPPG** (Wang et al., arXiv 2504.01774): repo licence **MIT**; ONNX model + state.json, step-by-step streaming on 36×36 face crops; trained on RLAP via PhysBench; browser demo licensed Apache-2.0 — [KegangWangCCNU/ME-rPPG](https://github.com/KegangWangCCNU/ME-rPPG); [ME-rPPG-demo](https://github.com/Health-HCI-Group/ME-rPPG-demo)
- **FacePhys**: release repo licence "Other"; arXiv paper CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; no code/weights release stated in paper; commercial cloud API and encrypted on-device SDK at facephys.com — [FacePhys-Release](https://github.com/KegangWangCCNU/FacePhys-Release); [arXiv 2512.06275](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06275v1); [FacePhys SDKs](https://github.com/FacePhys/FacePhys). Earlier project check: weights forbid server-side inference — `docs/model-feasibility.md` (local)
- **pyVHR** (phuselab): **GPL-3.0**, ~600 stars, last push Nov 2024 — [phuselab/pyVHR](https://github.com/phuselab/pyVHR)
- **PhysFormer** (CVPR 2022): MIT, last push Feb 2023 — [ZitongYu/PhysFormer](https://github.com/ZitongYu/PhysFormer)
- **RhythmFormer** (Pattern Recognition): MIT, last push Aug 2025 — [zizheng-guo/RhythmFormer](https://github.com/zizheng-guo/RhythmFormer)
- **PhysMamba** (CCBR 2024): GitHub reports **no licence** — [Chaoqi31/PhysMamba](https://github.com/Chaoqi31/PhysMamba)
- **VitalLens Python client**: MIT; estimates come from the VitalLens API; "general wellness purposes only" — [vitallens-python](https://github.com/Rouast-Labs/vitallens-python); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/vitallens)
- Contact-PPG encoders relevant to fingertip traces: PaPaGei (open PPG foundation model, 57,000 h / 20 M signals from public data) — [alphaXiv 2410.20542](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2410.20542); Samsung wavelet masked multiscale reconstruction (17 M segments from ~32,000 smartwatch users; Jan 2026) — [arXiv 2601.12215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12215v1); Apple wearable PPG/ECG foundation models (141,207 participants) — [arXiv 2312.05409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.05409). AnyPPG and Pulse-PPG are covered in `docs/model-feasibility.md`.

### Inferences
- Without a lawyer's reading, the rPPG-Toolbox RAIL "health and medical conditions" clause is a real risk for any app that reports health inferences; it is safest to use the toolbox for **evaluation** (dataset loaders, Bland-Altman metrics) and to deploy MIT-licensed architectures (PhysFormer, RhythmFormer code) retrained by us, or ME-rPPG weights.
- Released weights trained on UBFC-rPPG, PURE, MMPD, VitalVideo etc. may also inherit research-only dataset terms; MIT on a repo does not settle the dataset question.
- On the 4090, all of these models are tiny (≤ 7.4 M params); throughput is bounded by face detection and video decoding, not inference.
- A practical open server stack: face detection/landmarks → skin ROI crops → ensemble {POS, CHROM, OMIT, ME-rPPG, RhythmFormer/PhysFormer retrained} → SQI-gated median HR → peak detection with sub-sample interpolation → NeuroKit2/pyPPG HRV.

### Gaps
- RhythmMamba, FactorizePhys and iBVPNet repository licences were not checked.
- ME-rPPG weights' training data (RLAP) licence and any separate weight licence were not checked beyond the MIT repo licence.
- Whether pyVHR's bundled deep models (e.g., MTTS-CAN) carry separate licences was not checked.

## 5. What a server can compute from an uploaded fingertip trace or face video that a phone app cannot

### Takeaway
The server's advantages are heavier and multiple models (ensembling with signal-quality gating), re-processing archived raw data as better models appear, consistent cross-device calibration, and longitudinal personal baselines. The main risk is input quality: video compression at typical phone settings (CRF ≥ 22) breaks deep rPPG models, so the app should upload lossless face-ROI crops or per-frame RGB traces with frame timestamps rather than standard MP4s.

### Cited Findings
- Model cost spans three orders of magnitude: FacePhys 9.46 ms vs PhysFormer 2,330 ms CPU latency per clip — [arXiv 2512.06275v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06275v1); open-rppg exposes an SQI (0–1) per estimate, usable for gating — [open-rppg](https://github.com/KegangWangCCNU/open-rppg)
- Deep rPPG fails on H.264/H.265 at CRF ≥ 22 when trained on lossless data — [Sporrer 2024](https://library.imaging.org/ei/articles/36/10/IPAS-248); pulse SNR falls sharply with bitrate — [Nowara 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7899506)
- The VitalLens API itself operates on 40×40 px face crops prepared by the client, showing that small crops are sufficient input for a server model — [rouast.com/api](https://www.rouast.com/api/); ME-rPPG works on 36×36 crops — [ME-rPPG](https://github.com/KegangWangCCNU/ME-rPPG)
- Bae et al. selected the best of several ROIs by SNR and rejected low-SNR recordings, raising success from 83% (first try) to 95% (three tries) — [PMC9053269](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9053269/)
- Hoffman's SpO2 model is a CNN on mean-RGB traces from 3-s windows, i.e. it needs only per-frame channel means, not video — [arXiv 2104.00038](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2104.00038)
- rPPG-Toolbox provides standardised Bland-Altman evaluation and dataset loaders for benchmarking server models — [rPPG-Toolbox](https://github.com/ubicomplab/rPPG-Toolbox)
- Contact-PPG foundation encoders (PaPaGei, Samsung MMR, Apple) target many downstream tasks from PPG segments — [PaPaGei](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2410.20542); [arXiv 2601.12215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12215v1)

### Inferences
- **Fingertip**: upload per-frame R/G/B means (and ideally a few spatial ROIs) with sensor timestamps, exposure/ISO and torch state; the server can then (a) run multiple peak detectors with sub-sample interpolation for HRV, (b) compute SQI and reject segments, (c) run PPG encoders (AnyPPG/PaPaGei/Pulse-PPG) for embeddings and morphology features, (d) apply per-device colour calibration for any SpO2/Hb experiments, and (e) re-run everything later. The video itself is unnecessary for fingertip analysis.
- **Face**: upload a lossless (PNG/FFV1) or high-bitrate stream of stabilised face crops (e.g. 72–128 px) plus timestamps instead of the full-frame MP4; the server can then ensemble classical (POS/CHROM/OMIT) and deep models (ME-rPPG, retrained RhythmFormer/PhysFormer), take the SQI-weighted median, estimate RR from both rPPG and torso motion (requires keeping a torso crop), and cross-check face HR against simultaneous fingertip or watch HR.
- Longitudinal value: server-side storage permits personal baselines and drift detection (resting HR, rMSSD trends), which the literature supports better than absolute HRV or BP values.
- What the server cannot fix: motion, poor lighting, auto-exposure/tone-mapping applied before upload, and compression already baked into the file.

### Gaps
- No published study was found that directly compares on-device versus server-side ensembles for rPPG accuracy; the ensembling benefit is inferred, not measured.
- No study found on the optimal crop size and lossless codec for uploading face video for rPPG from Android phones; the 2026 ICIP study suggests resolution matters little but used different codecs.
